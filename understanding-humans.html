Understanding Humans

A working model of people — built the same first-principles way as the rest of this library, but pointed at other minds. Two lenses run through everything here: dependent origination (nothing arises by itself; assemble the causes and the effect must follow) and systems thinking (a person is stocks, flows and feedback loops). Learn to see with them, and people stop being mysterious — and life gets easier.

The bet

Underneath love and hate, friendship and rivalry, ambition and envy, there are only a few unchangeable root drives. Everything on the surface is those roots, expressed under different conditions. Understand the roots and the rules by which conditions shape them, and you can read almost anyone — including yourself.

The master lens

Dependent Origination

The single most useful idea for reading people: nothing comes from itself. No feeling, choice, or trait is self-caused — each is the effect of prior causes. And when sufficient causes come together, the effect isn’t a maybe — it must arise; it has no other option. So you never ask “what’s wrong with them?” You ask “what causes assembled to make this the inevitable result?”

01 · The mechanism
Assemble the causes → the effect must arise

A person’s temperament, their history, their state right now, how they read the moment, the incentive in play, the context — stack these up and the behaviour follows necessarily. Not chosen from nowhere; produced. This is why the same person acts differently in different conditions: change a cause, change the effect.

02 · Why it changes everything
From judging to understanding

“He’s just like that” is a dead end — it explains nothing and gives you no handle. Seeing the web of causes does two things: it dissolves blame (nobody chose their causes), and it hands you leverage — change a cause you can reach, and the effect shifts.

The takeaway

Stop looking for the villain or the flaw. Reconstruct the causes. When enough of them line up, anyone would do the same — and that’s exactly what lets you predict and gently change it.

A person is a system

The Human as a System

Model a human as stocks, flows and loops, not a fixed object. Inside are reservoirs (needs, beliefs, memories, mood, trust); things flow in (perceptions, experiences), things flow out (words, actions); and crucially, the outflow loops back — others react, and those reactions become new inflows. That feedback is why people spiral, harden, or grow.

The map
Inflows → inner stocks → outflows → and back

Behaviour isn’t a direct readout of a person — it’s the current level of their inner stocks meeting a situation. A tired, low-trust person and a rested, secure one produce different acts from the same trigger. And because reactions feed back with a delay, small nudges compound: treat someone as trustworthy and, over time, the loop can make them so.

The takeaway

Don’t read a single act as “who they are.” Read it as the state of their stocks right now, inside a feedback loop you’re part of. Change the inflow you control, and the loop does the rest.

Few roots, endless leaves

Roots & Expressions

Beneath the enormous variety of human feeling sits a small set of root drives — safety/survival, status, belonging, mating, meaning and fairness. Love, envy, loyalty, anger, pride, ambition are not separate things; they’re these roots expressed through particular conditions. Learn the roots, and the surface becomes readable.

01 · The structure
A handful of drives, expressed under conditions

Envy is status + scarcity. Loyalty is belonging + reciprocity. Anger is a threat to something a root cares about. Trace any behaviour down to which root it’s serving, and it stops being random. This is also the core unchangeable set whose expressions everything else is.

02 · Virtue vs. vice
The same drive, tuned by degree & context

Virtues and vices aren’t different substances — they’re the same drive at a different setting. Courage is the well-tuned middle between cowardice and recklessness; generosity between stinginess and profligacy. So “good” and “bad” character is largely a matter of calibration and conditions, not a fixed essence.

The takeaway

When someone acts, ask: which root, and at what setting? Naming the root explains the behaviour; naming the setting tells you whether it’s serving them or costing them.

Why they did that, now

Action in the Moment

A single action is the output of an equation: their state (needs, mood) × how they perceived the moment × the incentive in play × the context. Miss any term and the act looks irrational; include them all and it looks inevitable. And the term people most underweight is the incentive.

01 · The equation of an act
State × Perception × Incentive × Context

You rarely see the whole equation, so you fill the gap with a story about their character. Resist that. Ask what state they were in, how they read it (often wrongly), and what reward or threat was live. The act was the solution to their equation, not yours.

02 · Incentives
Toward reward, away from threat

Almost all behaviour is motion toward reward or away from threat — often social ones (approval, status, safety) more than material. So the fastest read on anyone is: what’s the incentive here? And the fastest way to change behaviour is to change the incentive, not to argue.

The takeaway

“Show me the incentive and I’ll show you the outcome.” To understand an act, find the reward it chased or the threat it fled — then you can predict the next one.

How connection is built

Bonds & Relationships

Bonds aren’t magic; they’re assembled from a few ingredients — proximity and repetition, reciprocity, vulnerability, shared reality, and safety — and then deepened by a loop: positive exchange builds trust, trust invites disclosure, disclosure brings closeness, closeness enables more positive exchange. Love, friendship, alliance and even hatred are just different positions in this exchange.

01 · The recipe
Ingredients → a bond → a reinforcing loop

You can literally build closeness on purpose: be around, give first, disclose a little, find shared reality, keep it safe. Each turn of the loop compounds. The same machinery run in reverse — broken reciprocity, threat, contempt — dissolves bonds just as reliably.

02 · The map of relationships
Exchange balance × closeness

Where a relationship sits depends on two axes: is the exchange net-benefit or net-threat, and how close/frequent is it. Love, friendship, alliance, rivalry and hatred are regions on that map — and people move across it as the exchange changes. Nothing is fixed.

The takeaway

Connection is a build, not a gift. Supply the ingredients and turn the loop, and closeness grows; let threat and broken reciprocity in, and it decays. Either way, it obeys rules you can use.

Born, then made

Nature & Conditioning

Two forces make a person: the inherent seeds they’re born with (temperament, the root drives) and the soil they grow in (environment, culture, experience). Neither alone decides them. Behaviour repeated becomes habit; habit hardens into character; character is what we then call someone’s “nature.” So “nature” is largely frozen behaviour — which means it can, slowly, be re-shaped.

The pipeline
Seeds + soil → repeated behaviour → habit → “nature”

What looks inborn is often just a deep groove worn by repetition in a particular environment. That’s hopeful: change the soil and the repeated behaviour, and over time even “that’s just who I am” bends. It’s also a warning: environments quietly manufacture character, in you and in others.

The takeaway

Separate the seed from the soil. You can’t swap someone’s temperament, but you (and they) can change the environment and the reps — and character follows, with a delay.

The levers people move by

Power & Influence

Influence isn’t manipulation trickery — it’s knowing the levers human nature already runs on. Reputation, status, reciprocity, scarcity, social proof, authority, presence and charm aren’t tricks; they’re the standing forces that shape how people respond to you. Seeing them lets you carry yourself deliberately — and makes you far harder to move against your interests.

The lever board
Where power and influence actually come from

Each lever is a root drive turned into a social force: status and reputation tap the status root; reciprocity and social proof tap belonging; authority and scarcity tap safety. Read a room by which levers are being pulled; build your own influence by earning the ones that compound — reputation and presence above all.

The takeaway

Influence is applied human nature. Learn the levers to move through the world with intent, protect yourself from those using them on you, and build the slow ones — reputation, aura, trust — that quietly outlast the rest.
