Staying in the loop

The Company Grapevine

I’m in the office only a day or two a week — usually one — and the friend who was my ear on the ground has left. Without an insider I’m flying blind on what’s happening: what’s hot, how review cycles work, what increments look like, who’s rising. This page is a plan to cultivate a reliable person I can talk to and ask, so I’m never out of the loop again.

The problem
Out of the loop is a real cost

One office day a week and no insider means I hear things late or never — and get blindsided at appraisal time or miss quiet opportunities. A single trusted, in-the-flow ally flips that: information comes to me early and honestly, and I can plan instead of react.

What to learn
The intel I actually want from them

Not gossip for its own sake — specific, decision-useful signal: when review cycles happen and what they reward, what the average increment/hike looks like, what’s currently hot and prioritised, re-orgs and who’s rising, the unwritten norms, and where projects are really headed.

The takeaway

One well-chosen, well-tended relationship replaces a whole information network. The rest of this page is how to pick that person, build the bond, keep a cadence I can sustain, and talk in a way that makes them want to share.

The right person

Who to Pick

The relationship is only as good as the person. You want someone in the flow of information, who enjoys sharing, keeps a confidence, and isn’t competing with you for the same rung.

The filter
Choose for reach + trust, not just friendliness

The best pick is often someone you already get on with who happens to be well-networked and long-tenured — start from existing warmth. Avoid the office gossip (they’ll leak you too), anyone as out-of-loop as you, and direct rivals for your role or rating.

The takeaway

Pick one or two people, not a crowd. Depth of trust beats breadth — a single person who tells you the truth is worth more than ten who tell you nothing.

Earning the relationship

Build the Bond

Connection is assembled, not lucky — even on one day a week. Supply the ingredients and turn the reciprocity loop, and a genuine two-way tie forms. (Same machinery as the Understanding Humans → Bonds section.)

The recipe
Give first, be safe, keep it two-way

On your office day, be present and consistent; give before you take (share something useful, grab a coffee, help with a small thing); show real interest in them, not just their information; be a little open yourself so it isn’t pure extraction; and above all be discreet — a vault. Trust compounds each cycle.

The takeaway

Never make them feel used. The person should feel they gain a warm, discreet, reciprocal friend — the information is a by-product of a real relationship, not the point of it.

How often

Cadence & Rhythm

The whole thing lives or dies on rhythm. With one office day a week, the trick is to make that day count in person and keep a light, steady thread going between visits — never bursts followed by silence.

The weekly beat
Deep in person + light async

Go the same day they’re in so you actually overlap, and spend real time together — lunch, coffee, a walk. Between visits, keep it warm with 1–2 low-stakes pings on Google Chat (a relevant link, a “how did X go?”, a small favour) — not a firehose. Warmth decays with silence, so a small steady minimum beats occasional floods.

The takeaway

Aim for both office days if you can; if it’s one, make it their day and protect a shared meal. Consistency is the signal — showing up predictably is what turns an acquaintance into an ally.

The conversation

How to Talk & What to Ask

How you ask decides whether they open up or clam up. Make it natural, mutual and unhurried — give a nugget, ask an open question, listen, react warmly, remember. Never interrogate, never leak.

The manner
Make it safe and two-way

Lead with something you know or a genuine question about them; ask open (“how’s the review cycle looking this time?”) not rapid-fire; and guard everything they tell you. Repeat their confidences once and the tap closes for good.

Review cycle
  • When does the next cycle actually kick off?
  • What are they really weighting this time?
  • Who decides my rating — manager, skip, committee?
  • What separated top-rated people last cycle?
Increments & hikes
  • What’s a typical increment for my band?
  • What did strong performers actually get?
  • Any freeze, budget squeeze, or bump this year?
  • Is a counter/negotiation normal here?
What’s hot
  • Which teams/projects have leadership’s attention?
  • What’s getting funded vs quietly wound down?
  • Where’s the growth — and where’s the risk?
Org & norms
  • Any re-orgs or leadership changes brewing?
  • Who’s genuinely rising, and why?
  • Unwritten rules — how things really get done here?

The takeaway

Ask for signal, not secrets. Keep it a friendly two-way exchange, protect their trust like your own, and over time you’ll always know what’s coming before it arrives.
