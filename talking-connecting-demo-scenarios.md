# Talking & Connecting — Demo Role-Plays (Read-Through Reference)

Eight scenarios, each played out as a full conversation between two fictional
people, so you can see the framework *in motion* before you're the one talking.
Every scenario is annotated inline with the **phase/step**, **ladder rung**,
**topic-map bucket**, and **active-listening move** in play, plus a short
debrief at the end naming the one highest-leverage thing to notice.

Difficulty ramps as you go down the list — easy 1:1s first, then status/stakes,
then curveballs, then a full Prime→Follow-up arc, then a "bad version vs fixed
version" of your two known failure modes (interview mode, going wide not deep).

Cast: **A** = the person practising the framework. **B / C** = the other
person(s), played realistically — flat answers, distraction, partial
reciprocity, all included on purpose. Realism > ease.

---

## 1. Bus to the offsite — 1:1, side-by-side, easy rep

**Setting:** Coach bus, two hours to the resort. A sits down next to B, who's
scrolling their phone. Formation: solo, seated, captive — about as easy as
APPROACH gets.

> **A:** *(sitting, phone away, small nod)* "This the only bus going, or are
> there like three of them?"
> *[OPEN — obvious thing about right now, not a personal question yet]*
>
> **B:** "Think there's one more leaving in twenty. We got the early one
> though, so — fewer people, more legroom." *(half-smile, still on phone)*
>
> **A:** "Ha, smart. I just followed whoever looked most awake at 7am." *(reacts
> + adds a crumb of self, doesn't fire the next question immediately)*
> *[Active listening move 5 — responded to her line before adding own]*
>
> **B:** *(actual laugh, puts phone down)* "That's a real strategy actually."
>
> **A:** "What team are you on, by the way?"
> *[Ladder rung: Openers → about to test Work & gossip]*
>
> **B:** "Platform infra. You?"
>
> **A:** "Growth. So you're the reason nothing goes down and nobody thanks
> you for it."
>
> **B:** *(energy jump — sits up slightly)* "Oh my god, literally. We had a
> whole incident last month where—" *(launches into story)*
> *[SPARK — the energy jump. A should drop other probes now and dig here.]*
>
> **A:** "Wait, what happened?" *(follows the live wire, doesn't change
> subject)*
> *[SPARK step 3 done right — deeper, not wider]*
>
> **B:** *(tells a genuine on-call war story, animated now)* "...and the
> worst part is nobody even noticed until finance flagged it."
>
> **A:** "That's such a specific kind of pain — being the only one who knows
> the building's on fire." *(reflects the feeling, not just the facts)*
> *[Active listening — reflect-back before adding own]*
>
> **B:** "Exactly! Okay what about you, any growth-team horror stories?"
> *(reciprocity — B is now asking back)*
>
> **A:** *(trades a matched-depth story — not deeper than this, just as real)*

**Debrief:** Textbook OPEN→SPARK. The moment worth noticing: A didn't ask "what
team are you on" as item 1 of a checklist — it came after a real react. And
when B's energy visibly jumped on the infra story, A didn't say "cool, so
what do you do outside work" (going wide) — A stayed on the live wire
(going deep). **Cue:** the energy jump is the only signal that matters;
everything else is just casting.

## 2. Half-full dinner table — reading the formation before you sit

**Setting:** Buffet dinner, one table has four people mid-conversation, laughing;
the next table has two people eating quietly, not talking to each other, one
looking at their phone. A is holding a plate, deciding where to sit.

**Read-the-room, before any dialogue:**
- Table 1 (four, animated) = deep, entrenched conversation. Joining cold
  means interrupting a peak, not a lull. **Wait, or hover at the edge.**
- Table 2 (two, quiet, one on phone) = an *open* formation — no conversation
  to break into, low stakes, easy entry. **This is the approach.**
- *[APPROACH — read the formation before moving your feet]*

> **A:** *(sits down, one seat of space, not crowding)* "Is the paneer
> actually good or is everyone just being polite about the offsite food?"
> *[OPEN — the shared, obvious thing: the food in front of everyone]*
>
> **C:** *(puts phone down, laughs)* "Honestly? Better than I expected. The
> dessert's the real win though."
>
> **D:** *(the quiet one, looks up)* "I haven't tried it yet."
>
> **A:** "Okay that's your assignment before we leave this table." *(light,
> includes D without putting her on the spot)*
>
> **D:** *(small smile)* "Deal."

**Debrief:** The read itself was the whole skill here — A didn't need a clever
opener, just the correct formation call. **Cue:** when in doubt about where to
sit, look for the pair of quiet people before the loud group of four. Loud
groups are fun to watch, terrible to join cold.

## 3. Tea break, 1:1 — SPARK into DEEPEN, reciprocity check

**Setting:** Outside, tea break between sessions. A and E ended up at the same
spot. This is the rep that actually tests DEEPEN, not just SPARK.

> **A:** "You looked like you were taking really detailed notes in there —
> are you actually going to use that framework or is it just conference
> theatre?" *(light, a little cheeky — Tech & AI bucket)*
>
> **E:** *(laughs)* "Half and half, honestly. I actually want to try it on
> our onboarding flow."
>
> **A:** "What's broken about onboarding right now?"
> *[still SPARK — following the thread, not yet DEEPEN]*
>
> **E:** "It's not broken, it's just... nobody owns it. Every team assumes
> someone else is watching the drop-off numbers."
>
> **A:** "That sounds like it's not really a metrics problem — sounds like a
> nobody-wants-to-own-the-boring-thing problem." *(A goes first with an
> observation that's a little more real, testing E's reaction)*
> *[DEEPEN step — shift facts → meaning, go first]*
>
> **E:** *(pause, then more honest)* "Yeah... honestly that's most of my job
> right now. I took this role because I wanted to build stuff, and I'm
> mostly just chasing people to care about a dashboard."
> *(E matched the depth — reciprocity confirmed)*
>
> **A:** "That's a rough gap between what you signed up for and what you're
> doing." *(reflects the feeling, doesn't rush to fix or advise)*
>
> **E:** "It is. I haven't said that out loud to anyone here yet, actually."
> *[Signal: this just earned a rung — Interests → edge of Personal]*
>
> **A:** *(doesn't push further right there — notices the container is wrong:
> a tea break with people walking past)* "Honestly, that's a real
> conversation, not a five-minute-tea-break one. You around later tonight?"
> *[DEEPEN's last move — follow the feeling to a better container]*
>
> **E:** "Yeah, actually — let's do that."

**Debrief:** The highest-leverage moment: A noticed the conversation had
outgrown its container (a rushed tea break) and *named that* instead of either
(a) awkwardly cutting E off, or (b) forcing the whole heavy topic into five
standing-up minutes. **Cue:** DEEPEN's job isn't to go as deep as possible on
the spot — it's to recognize when depth wants a better room and move it there.

## 4. Someone senior, standing alone — status raises the stakes

**Setting:** A VP nobody really talks to outside meetings is standing by the
railing alone, drink in hand, not on their phone. Higher stakes than scenario 2
because of status, not because the mechanics are different.

**Read-the-room:** Solo, not on phone, not scanning to leave — approachable.
The trap here is over-preparing an "impressive" opener. *[Premise: curiosity
beats cleverness, especially with seniority — they get pitched at all day.]*

> **A:** *(approaches at an angle, not head-on)* "This is a nice view for a
> conference center parking lot, somehow." *(light, self-aware, no attempt
> to sound smart)*
> *[OPEN — the obvious thing, deliberately unimpressive]*
>
> **F:** *(small laugh)* "Right? I didn't expect that either."
>
> **A:** "Is this your first one of these, or have you done a few?" *(easy,
> low-stakes, not a status question)*
>
> **F:** "Done a bunch. They kind of blur together after a while, not gonna
> lie."
>
> **A:** *(resists the urge to ask something "smart" about strategy — casts
> a normal probe instead)* "Yeah? What's actually different about a good one
> versus a forgettable one, for you?"
> *[SPARK probe — genuinely curious, not testing him]*
>
> **F:** *(actually thinks about it)* "Honestly — whether I talk to anyone
> below my own directs. Most of these I just end up in the same five
> conversations."
> *(energy shift — this is F's live wire)*
>
> **A:** "So this is statistically already going well for you, then." *(reacts
> with a crumb of humor, doesn't overplay the moment)*
>
> **F:** *(genuine laugh)* "It is, actually. What team are you on?"
> *(F reciprocating — good sign)*

**Debrief:** The failure mode this scenario is designed to catch is
over-preparing — trying to sound impressive to someone senior. A's opener was
deliberately small. **Cue:** with status differences, unimpressive and curious
beats polished every time — seniority doesn't change the mechanics, it just
raises your temptation to perform.

## 5. Group mid-laugh — the hardest APPROACH read

**Setting:** Four people by the bar, clearly mid in-joke, laughing hard. A
doesn't know the joke. This is the scenario most people get wrong by either
barging in or avoiding the group entirely.

> *(A walks up, doesn't ask "what's funny" — that puts the group on the
> hook to explain a joke to a stranger, which usually kills it)*
>
> **A:** *(stands at the edge, genuinely smiling at their energy, waits for
> a natural gap)* "Okay I have no idea what happened but I need to know if
> I should be worried." *(light, self-aware, gives them an easy way to
> loop A in without a full explain)*
> *[APPROACH into OPEN — names the obvious situation: I'm outside this joke]*
>
> **G:** *(laughing, waving A in)* "No no, it's dumb — H tried to order in
> Hindi and accidentally ordered four of something nobody wanted."
>
> **A:** "Say no more, I respect the commitment to the bit even when it
> backfires." *(reacts warmly, doesn't ask H to re-explain the whole story)*
>
> **H:** *(grinning)* "It was ONE word wrong!"
>
> **A:** "What'd you actually get?"
> *[small OPEN follow-up — keeps riding the existing energy rather than
> introducing a new topic]*

**Debrief:** The move that made this work: A didn't ask "what's funny," which
forces someone to explain a joke (usually kills it), and didn't stand outside
looking left out either. A named the situation honestly and lightly, which
gave the group an easy, low-effort way to pull A in. **Cue:** joining a
laughing group is an OPEN about the laugh itself, not a request to explain the
laugh.

## 6. Curveball — the one-word-answer person

**Setting:** A tries to talk to I, who gives short, flat answers. Tests
whether A can read low reciprocity and warm-exit instead of forcing it.

> **A:** "Hey, how's the offsite treating you so far?"
>
> **I:** "Fine."
>
> **A:** "Yeah? Anything stood out, or pretty standard so far?"
>
> **I:** "Not really."
>
> **A:** *(one more honest probe, not five)* "Fair enough — sometimes these
> things are just fine, no shame in that."
> *[testing reciprocity once more, lightly, not interrogating]*
>
> **I:** "Yeah, exactly." *(no opening offered)*
>
> **A:** *(reads it — no spark after 2-3 honest tries, doesn't force a
> fourth question)* "Well, I'm gonna go raid the dessert table before it's
> gone — good talking to you." *(warm exit, no thread needed because there's
> nothing to hook)*
> *[Correctly accepting "no spark yet" instead of forcing chemistry]*
>
> **I:** "Ha, good call."

**Debrief:** The skill being tested is *stopping*, not pushing harder. A gave
it two honest attempts past the first flat answer, felt no lift, and exited
warm instead of interrogating a fourth time. **Cue:** forcing chemistry reads
worse than accepting none — a warm exit after 2-3 tries keeps the door open
for a day-2 crossing; a fourth forced question closes it.

## 7. Curveball — the person who dominates

**Setting:** J has been talking for four straight minutes about their own
project. A's real test here is active listening under the urge to interject,
not talking more.

> **J:** *(mid-monologue)* "...and then we had to rewrite the whole service
> because the first version couldn't handle the load, which honestly nobody
> tells you happens until it happens to you, and then on top of that the
> vendor changed their pricing model right in the middle of—"
>
> *(A has three things they want to say: a similar story, a question about
> the vendor, and a joke. A tags each with one word — "vendor," "joke,"
> "story" — and lets them pass rather than jumping in.)*
> *[Active listening move 3 — ride the urge, tag and release]*
>
> **J:** "—so yeah, it was a whole month of just firefighting."
> *(finally pauses)*
>
> **A:** *(one beat of silence, then responds to J first, not with the story
> A was holding)* "The vendor changing pricing mid-crisis is almost funnier
> than it should be — like the universe wanted to test you twice." *(reflect
> the feeling + a light reaction, before adding own thing)*
> *[Active listening move 5 — respond to them before adding own]*
>
> **J:** *(actually laughs, relaxes)* "Right?! Anyway sorry, I've been going
> on — what about you, anything like that on your side?"
> *(J self-corrects and reciprocates once actually heard)*
>
> **A:** *(now trades the story A held onto, since J invited it)*

**Debrief:** The tempting move was to jump in with A's own vendor story the
moment J mentioned pricing — that would have been a topic hijack, not a
reply. Tagging it and waiting let J finish, and the reflect-back is what
actually made J self-correct and open a door back to A. **Cue:** with a
dominator, the fix isn't competing for airtime — it's listening well enough
that they run out of momentum and hand you the floor themselves.

## 8. Full arc — Prime through Follow-up, compressed

**Setting:** Day 1 mixer activity (a group trek), A meets K. This one runs the
whole lifecycle in one scenario so you can see how the steps actually chain.

**PRIME (before, narrated):** A skimmed their deck on the walk over — tagged
the "travel mishap" story to "if travel comes up," and set a light aim: meet
a few people, don't force it.

> **APPROACH:** *(trekking group naturally pairs up on the trail; A ends up
> beside K, an open, side-by-side formation — no need to "break in")*
>
> **OPEN — A:** "Is it just me or is this trail steeper than the itinerary
> made it sound?" *(the obvious shared thing: the trek itself)*
>
> **K:** "Genuinely misled. I brought the wrong shoes."
>
> **A:** "Bold strategy. I overprepared and now I have three layers I don't
> need." *(reacts + crumb of self)*
>
> **SPARK — A:** "You do this kind of thing often — treks, outdoorsy stuff?"
> *(casting a probe from the Topic Map: Fitness & sport / Travel)*
>
> **K:** *(energy lifts)* "Actually yeah — I did a trek in Spiti last year
> that went really wrong in a good way."
>
> **A:** *(drops other probes, follows the live wire)* "Wrong how?"
>
> **K:** *(tells the story — got stuck an extra night, ended up at a random
> homestay, one of their best travel memories)*
>
> **DEEPEN — A:** "It's funny how the trips that go wrong are always the
> ones you actually remember." *(shifts from facts to meaning, goes first
> with a small real observation)*
>
> **K:** "Totally — my planned trips barely register. That one I still think
> about, like, monthly." *(K matches — a little more honest than needed)*
>
> **A:** "What was it about that night specifically?"
> *(one notch further, letting K lead the depth)*
>
> **K:** "I think it's that I had zero control and it turned out fine anyway.
> I don't let myself feel that at work, ever."
> *(genuine, a rung up toward Personal)*
>
> **HOOK — A:** *(trail is ending, group reconverging — A spots the close
> point)* "Okay that's staying with me. I'm terrible at the zero-control
> thing too — remind me tonight, I want to hear the rest of that Spiti
> story properly." *(exits on the high, names a specific thread, sets up
> reconnecting)*
>
> **K:** "Deal — find me at dinner."

**FOLLOW-UP (the week after, narrated):**
> *Message sent 30 hours later:* "Hey! Still thinking about the Spiti
> homestay story — you never got to the part about how you got back down.
> Also if you're ever doing another trek like that, I'm in." *(specific,
> references a detail, delivers on the named thread)*

**Debrief:** Notice the whole arc took under ten minutes of trail-talk and
never left "trekking" — that's depth-not-coverage in miniature. The DEEPEN
question ("what was it about that night specifically") is the single highest-
leverage line in the whole scenario: it's what turned a fun travel story into
something K hadn't said out loud before. **Cue:** one good "what was that
actually like for you" question does more than five follow-up facts.

## 9. Bad version vs. fixed version — your two known failure modes

Same scenario, run twice: once showing the failure mode, once fixed. This is
the one to reread when you catch yourself mid-slip.

### 9a. Interview mode

> **A:** "What do you do?"
> **L:** "I'm in design, on the consumer app."
> **A:** "Oh cool, how long have you been there?"
> **L:** "About two years."
> **A:** "Did you study design or switch into it?"
> **L:** *(flatter each time)* "Switched, from architecture."
> **A:** "Interesting, why'd you switch?"
> **L:** "...it's a long story." *(visibly checking out of the conversation)*

**What went wrong:** every A line was a new question with zero reaction or
self-disclosure in between — a survey, not a conversation. L never got
anything back to react to, so L's answers got shorter, not longer.

### 9b. Fixed version — same opener, active listening added

> **A:** "What do you do?"
> **L:** "I'm in design, on the consumer app."
> **A:** "Oh nice — I always find design roles a little mysterious, everyone
> seems to end up there sideways." *(reacts with a real thought, not just a
> question)*
> **L:** *(slight smile)* "That's exactly what happened to me actually, I
> was in architecture before this."
> **A:** "Wait, architecture to app design — what was the actual bridge
> there?" *(now genuinely curious because L handed A a thread, not a
> scripted next question)*
> **L:** *(more animated)* "Honestly, it's mostly the same skill — you're
> just designing for a screen instead of a building. But it took me a while
> to see that."
> **A:** "That's such a good reframe." *(reflects, doesn't rush to the next
> fact)*

**Debrief:** Identical topic, identical first question — the only difference
is A reacting and adding a crumb of self before each new question in 9b. That
single habit is what turns a Q&A into a conversation. **Cue:** if you notice
your last three lines were all questions, you're in interview mode — the fix
isn't a better question, it's a reaction before the next one.

---

## What to take from all nine

- The mechanics repeat: **read the formation → obvious opener → cast, don't
  survey → follow the live wire → go first on depth → exit on the high →
  close the loop within 48h.**
- Active listening isn't a tenth scenario — it's the layer that made 3, 7,
  8, and 9b work at all.
- The two curveballs (6, 7) are really testing restraint: stopping when
  there's no spark, and staying quiet long enough to let someone else run
  out of steam.
