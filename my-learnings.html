My Learnings · One Algorithm, Many Doors

The MAYA Loop

Most Advanced Yet Acceptable. One loop runs underneath taste, media, feeds, conversation, flirting, storytelling, fashion — and Bayes. Each domain is a different door into the same machine.

Breadth builds a network → association proposes a move → MAYA picks the one that's new-yet-safe → you change one thing → the result updates you → the win becomes your new normal → repeat. These aren't analogies to each other. They're the same algorithm wearing different clothes.

One loop, many doors. Every branch is a separate entrance into the same five-stage machine — the whole claim of this note, in one picture.

◆

The Radar — One Shape, One Knob at a Time

Anything you're getting to know — a person, a taste, a wardrobe — is a shape across several axes. You can't read it all at once, so you extend one spoke at a time: turn a single knob, watch the reaction, and because only that axis moved, the change is attributable to it. Turn the knobs and watch a shape emerge.

Every spoke starts at the centre — you know nothing yet.

The emergent polygon is your map — the prior in Part 09. One-knob-at-a-time is why the map can be trusted: each extension is one clean, attributable experiment. "Understanding" is the whole shape, never a single axis.

🕸️ Why a radar, not a bar chart
01

The Curve — Most Advanced Yet Acceptable

Coined by industrial designer Raymond Loewy. People crave novelty and fear the unfamiliar at the same time. The winning move sits at the crossover: as new as possible while still feeling safe.

Two failure modes, one target. Slide too far left and it's forgettable; too far right and it's refused. The peak is the most new a thing can be while still landing as safe.

🌍 Where MAYA shows up
02

Explore vs Exploit

Pure MAYA only takes small steps — it deepens one vein and traps you in a local maximum. Taste-building needs two moves: reliable increments, and rare deliberate jumps out of the safe zone.

Optimal strategy: mostly-MAYA increments, punctuated by low-frequency jumps. Exploit is reliable but caps out; explore has a high failure rate but is the only route to a genuinely new region. This is the explore/exploit tradeoff straight out of reinforcement learning.

EExploit

Incremental MAYA steps out from what you already love. Low variance, reliable payoff — you get steadily better at a thing you're already into. Left alone forever, it strands you on a hill that isn't the mountain.

XExplore

A full jump outside the acceptable zone. Most jumps miss — that's the cost. But the ones that land don't just add a point; they open an entire new axis you can then exploit for months.

The Real Trick — Break One Axis, Hold Another

A leap that lands is almost never zero-familiarity. It's radical on one dimension while familiar on another. Changing one thing while tethering another is what lets a "too advanced" thing become acceptable.

Move up, not diagonally. Push hard on the axis you break, but stay anchored on the one you keep. Only the top-right cell lands — the tether is what turns novelty into something legible instead of rejected.

04

The Dark Mirror — When the Feed Runs It on You

Instagram, TikTok, YouTube run a MAYA-shaped loop on you automatically: same topic, slightly new flavor, tuned to keep the dopamine going. Same engine, opposite goal.

Why it persists. The feed isn't chasing the MAYA peak — it's a reinforcing loop: more engagement → a narrower niche → a sharper dopamine hit → more engagement. Every arrow is positive, so the groove only tightens. When you run the loop you insert the explore step — the one move that turns the spiral outward instead.

You run it

A map of yourself

Clip → other sitcoms → animated → romance — but you decide when to jump axes. Each landed leap is a fact you learned about your own taste.

The feed runs it

An ever-narrower groove

Same walk, but steered to maximize engagement, not to widen you. The novelty is real; the direction is chosen for the platform. Ever-narrower is a feature, not a bug.

05

Conversation as a MAYA Walk

Each turn stays tethered to the last but adds one new flavor — never caged (boring), never a non-sequitur (jarring). The skilled move is the same one: shift one dimension while holding another.

The walk, plus one branch. Most turns nudge a single knob. Occasionally you fire a cheap off-topic probe; if it misses, you drop it without clinging. Exploration is only cheap if you don't cling to a dead reference.

aOne knob is diagnostic

Escalating one lever isn't just smoother — if it lands, you learn something specific. Move three at once and a hit teaches you nothing about which worked. Same reason a clean experiment isolates variables.

bThe probe skill is the abandonment

The hard part isn't firing the off-topic probe — it's dropping it the instant it misses. Cheap exploration depends entirely on a light grip on any single reference.

The assembled loop. "Reading a room" decomposes into this cycle — each pass re-anchors and turns again. The rest is reps: procedural memory, not declarative.

The assembled loop

Walk → detect resonance → isolate the live element → escalate one knob → occasionally probe → keep what lands / drop what doesn't → resume from the new anchor.

This is most of what "reading a room" decomposes into. The rest is reps — it's procedural memory, not declarative. You can grasp it in one read, but only feel the timing after hundreds of turns.

06

Association + MAYA = Generator + Selector

Association is the generator: from the current topic your mind throws up adjacent nodes. MAYA is the selector: of everything firing, which one is advanced-yet-acceptable? Association without MAYA is scattershot; MAYA without association has nothing interesting to place.

Breadth is upstream of everything. Every domain you absorb adds nodes — and, crucially, edges between distant nodes. Edge-density is the real mechanism behind being "quick": it lets the engine generate a MAYA-legal pivot from almost any topic to almost any other.

Generator

Association

Dissociates the topic into elements and throws up adjacent nodes. Rich, fast, undirected. On its own: non-sequiturs.

Selector

MAYA

Picks the one candidate that's new-yet-safe. Directed, tasteful, narrow. On its own: safe but has nothing interesting to place in the band.

Why it impresses people involuntarily

Pivoting on one shared element sends a double signal: "I'm following you (safe) and taking you somewhere (novel)."

The brain reads that as social intelligence before judgment engages. The five-stage loop in full: breadth builds the graph → association traverses it → MAYA selects the legal move → one-knob escalation executes → feedback re-anchors.

One machine, five stages. Every domain in this note is an entrance to this exact cycle.

07

Premise vs Craft — the Threshold and the Variable

Premise is a threshold, not the variable. It only has to clear a bar — non-offensive, "good enough." Above the bar it stops mattering and craft takes over. Craft is applied relentlessly, one turn at a time.

Plain-premise works prove it: Breaking Bad, Monster, Hunter × Hunter, Better Call Saul — the premise was never the point; the obsession is craft. Done well, craft doesn't just save a weak premise — a great cold open authors premise, manufacturing the felt sense of "there's something here" in minutes.

The storyteller's asymmetry

A partner is a co-author, not an audience

A novelist controls both sides. In conversation the other person is live. Run frameworks at someone and perceptive people feel the absence of genuine reception. The peak moment is when they generate a contribution and you follow it — giving up control on purpose.

The invisibility clause

Presence is what keeps the craft invisible

Presence and empathy aren't extra frameworks — they're what keep the others hidden. The craft reads as magic rather than manipulation only when it runs underneath actually caring about the answer. The magic is the invisibility.

The Wardrobe — a Worked Application

Essentials — well-fitted tees, good denim, a neutral base — are premise: clear the gate, then go quiet. But "boring" is a failure too (the too-safe end of the curve), so the base must clear the bar on the invisible dimensions: fit, fabric weight, collar, exact shade.

One knob at a time, in radar form. Fit, texture, proportion and formality held at your baseline (dashed); only Colour extended this round. Because a single spoke moved, a hit or miss is fully attributable — and the shape that builds up over months is your wardrobe's map.

1Build on fit & material first

Not item category. A "boring" base still has to be right on the invisible axes — the too-safe end of the curve is a failure mode, not a safe harbor.

2Make the knob abstract

Change a dimension (proportion, colour temperature, formality, texture, silhouette), not a product. "Warm earth tones suit me" teaches a whole region; "this brown jacket works" teaches one jacket.

3Re-anchor on a win

A combination that lands and stops feeling costumey gets promoted to the new base. MAYA runs from there.

4The band walks outward

Over a year, your "essentials" become things that would've read as too advanced twelve months earlier. Same loop as taste — the acceptable zone migrates.

The Bayesian Substrate

All of the above is one thing: credit assignment under uncertainty. Perturbing a single variable is the cleanest way to attribute an outcome to a cause — which is exactly what Bayesian updating formalizes.

The loop, written out formally. The perturbation is one attributable piece of evidence; the reaction is the observed outcome; promoting the win is the posterior becoming the next prior. That promotion is identical to re-anchoring taste and to promoting a landed outfit.

Why two knobs eventually work

A strong prior is the hedge

Early, with a flat prior, you must go one at a time — no model to attribute against. Once you've run enough single-variable experiments, you have a predictive model: move two knobs, predict knob A from the model, and attribute the residual to B. A strong prior converts a two-variable mess back into a one-variable question.

The thing to guard

Interaction effects

The only reason two-knob tests are ever informative is that real systems have interactions — A and B together do something neither does alone (a bold jacket that works with slim trousers and dies with wide). Single-variable testing maps main effects; two-knob testing maps interactions — and you can never skip the single-variable phase, because interactions are only interpretable against a model of what each part does alone.

The Unifying Claim — & Where It Breaks

The unifying claim

MAYA, explore/exploit, conversation, association, storytelling, fashion and Bayes are not analogies to each other — they are instances of one algorithm: breadth builds a network → association generates candidate moves → MAYA selects the advanced-yet-acceptable one → a single-variable perturbation executes it → feedback updates the prior → the posterior becomes the new base → repeat, with two-variable experiments unlocked once the prior is strong enough to hedge and isolate interactions.

Bayes = the formal write-upPresence = the invisibility layerBreadth = the raw material

Held to its own standard, the framework is strongest where it stays a reinforcement-learning / cognitive-graph claim, and weakest where it reaches for Bayes and for "everything is one algorithm." Four honest cracks:

Four bones off one claim. Each is a contributing weakness in "one algorithm for everything" — detailed in the cards below.

Crack 01 · falsifiability

It can absorb any outcome

A hit? MAYA-legal, one knob, strong prior, presence present. A miss? You moved two knobs, the prior was weak, or presence was absent. If no observation could embarrass it, it's a lens, not a model. The repair is to force a losable prediction.

Crack 02 · Bayes vs OFAT

The formal layer undercuts itself

"Single-variable is the only clean way" is not what experimental statistics says — one-factor-at-a-time is the classically criticised design, and DOE varies factors together from the start. The honest version is narrower: with a flat prior and no parallel trials, one-at-a-time is a pragmatic concession, not a law.

Crack 03 · the dark mirror

Feeds don't target the peak

MAYA is a claim about a peak. Engagement optimisers climb whatever maximises watch-time — often the low-novelty comfort end or the extreme end, the opposites of "advanced yet acceptable." Same generator, different objective — and the objective is what defines MAYA.

Crack 04 · storytelling

Survivorship

Breaking Bad et al. are selected on success; we never see the brilliantly-crafted plain-premise works that died. And "craft authors premise" is one slip from circular. The defensible core survives: craft has a higher ceiling, premise has a floor — but "premise is only a threshold" over-counts the winners.

The through-line

Shared vocabulary is cheap. The real test is shared predictions.

The unification currently does more aesthetic work than explanatory work — it feels unified because the vocabulary is shared. Give it one prediction that could lose, and it graduates from an elegant lens to a model.

A living note · built from the MAYA / Single-Variable Loop framework · engage with it, don't just affirm it.

The reach of the curve

Where MAYA shows up

Anywhere preference and adoption meet. Not just industrial design:

  • Products & tech — the new gadget that feels inevitable, not alien.
  • Music & film / series — genre loyalty is a MAYA band.
  • Branding, language, architecture — the familiar reworded just enough.
  • Algorithms & feeds — the automated, relentless version (see the Dark Mirror).
  • Conversation & flirting — escalation as a walk along the curve.
Explore payoff · worked example

The Friends leap

The taste was narrow: heavy psychological / philosophical prestige TV, thrillers, sci-fi, documentaries. Sitcoms sat in the "too advanced for my taste" zone — paradoxically, the unfamiliar region.

Taking a deliberate leap on Friends landed. It opened a whole new region: other sitcoms, animated sitcoms, romantic subplots. That single leap was the explore payoff — a new axis that months of exploit could then walk along.

The leap worked because it wasn't zero-familiarity — it broke one axis (pure comedy, no cliffhangers) while holding another (elite writing, character depth). That's Part 03.
Pressure-test

Do feeds actually run MAYA?

The line "the feed runs MAYA on you but suppresses explore" is gorgeous — and it smuggles in a claim that the feed optimises the same quantity you do, just inverted.

It doesn't. MAYA targets a peak between novelty-hunger and unfamiliarity-fear. An engagement optimiser climbs whatever maximises watch-time — empirically the low-novelty comfort loop or the outrage/shock extreme, the exact opposites of "advanced yet acceptable."
What survives: same generator (small perturbations from a profile), different objective. The mechanism resembles MAYA but isn't identical — the feed just sometimes passes through the band on its way elsewhere.
A special case

Flirting = escalation as a MAYA walk

Flirting is just the conversational walk with intimacy as the escalating knob. Each move stays tethered (safe) while nudging one dimension up (novel).

The two sub-rules matter most here: escalate one lever so a landing is diagnostic, and treat every probe as cheap — fire it lightly, and if it misses, drop it without a flinch. Clinging to a dead reference is what turns a probe into a cringe.

Craft over premise

Plain-premise masterpieces

Works whose loglines are unremarkable, carried entirely by craft applied one turn at a time:

  • Breaking Bad — a man makes and sells drugs.
  • Monster — a doctor chases a patient he saved.
  • Hunter × Hunter — a boy looks for his father.
  • Better Call Saul — a lawyer cuts corners.

Front-loaded-scale works (One Piece, LOTR) do the opposite — they borrow the "this will be big" feeling early. Either way, premise was never the variable.

Pressure-test

Survivorship

The plain-premise exemplars are all winners, drawn from a population where the brilliantly-crafted plain-premise flops are invisible — we never heard of them.

"Craft authors premise" is one definitional slip from circular: if it succeeded, premise didn't matter; if it failed, the craft wasn't good enough. Unlosable. And "chemistry teacher cooks meth" is itself a high-concept hook, not a threshold-clearer.
The defensible core: craft has a higher ceiling, premise has a floor. That's true and useful. "Premise is only a threshold" is stronger than the evidence allows.
Worked application

The wardrobe walk

  • Set the base. Fit and fabric first — a tee/denim/neutral base that clears the invisible bar.
  • Turn one dial. Hold watch, frames, bottom, shoes; change only the top. A hit is attributable.
  • Prefer abstract dials. "Warm earth tones" over "this brown jacket" — you learn a region, not an item.
  • Re-anchor. A combo that lands and stops feeling costumey becomes the new base.
  • Watch the band migrate. A year later, today's "too advanced" is next year's essentials.
Pressure-test · the juiciest

Is this Bayes — or OFAT?

The Bayes mapping escalates from "is like" to "is an instance of," but there's no likelihood function, no distribution over outcomes, no computable posterior. Calling one-knob perturbation "the likelihood" is metaphor wearing identity's clothes.

Worse, it undercuts itself. "Single-variable is the only clean attribution" is one-factor-at-a-time (OFAT) — the design experimental statistics criticises. Design of Experiments varies factors together from the start because factorial designs extract more per trial and are the only economical way to map interactions. The framework arrives at DOE's conclusion (interactions matter) while its stated method is what DOE replaced.
The honest, narrower claim survives: for a human who can't run 16 parallel trials, with a flat prior, isolating one variable is the pragmatic move. Downgrade "instance of Bayes" to "shares the credit-assignment problem with Bayes."
Dimensional

Why a radar, not a bar chart

A bar chart ranks independent scores. But the axes of a person — or a taste, or an outfit — aren't independent, and you don't care about the ranking. You care about the shape.

The radar makes three things visible at once:

  • One knob at a time. Extend a single spoke and the silhouette changes in exactly one place — the change is attributable (Part 09).
  • The emergent gestalt. Understanding isn't any one axis; it's the whole polygon — the map, the prior.
  • Interactions. Two spokes pulled together make a shape neither makes alone — precisely what the two-knob phase maps.
My Learnings · Craft & Systems

Development > Premise

The seed is handed to you. The growth is craft. Excellence isn’t in the starting point — it’s in everything you build on top, and in being pointedly great at a coherent few things rather than evenly good at all.

The premise is just the seed. The craft is everything that grows from it — and it’s almost always the real variable.

02

Pointed Excellence Beats Even Adequacy

You don’t need every channel. Be outstanding on the ones your audience weights highest — a few loud knobs, coherently aimed — and you win the people who value them.

Adequate-at-everything is forgettable. That’s why Witch Hat bounced off — nothing turned up loud enough to be for someone specifically.
03

Two Edges — Where the Real Skill Lives

The thesis is true, but not unconditionally. These two edges keep it honest.

Edge 01 — Floors still exist

Craft isn’t free, and some seeds are salted ground

Some premises carry a structural flaw no development overcomes. And craft is the thing you spend years building — the person with a dull premise and no craft isn’t saved by this principle, they’re indicted by it.

Any start is good enough — for someone who did the work to earn the craft. Don’t let “the start doesn’t matter” become permission to skip the part that does.
Edge 02 — Coherence is required

Strong knobs one way · weak knobs kept quiet

JoJo’s weak realism doesn’t hurt — nothing was trying to be realistic, so the weak channel is silent, not fighting. But a weak channel that contradicts the strong one — gorgeous visuals on a confusing story, charisma undercut by visible dishonesty — is corrosive.

Be great at a coherent few, and keep the rest at least non-sabotaging.
A silent weakness is neutral. A weakness pulling against your strength is corrosive.
04

The Fully-Loaded Thesis

The version worth signing

Excellence is built in development, not handed to you in the premise — and it comes from being pointedly great at a coherent few things while keeping your weak points from actively working against you. Not from being evenly good at everything.

The Trap of Perfectionism

Perfectionism is the unreasonable, self-defeating ambition of getting something absolutely right — which makes us hard to be around and punishing to live within. The fault isn’t aiming high. It’s under-budgeting for how hard high actually is.

▶ Watch · School of Life: premature perfectionism ↗
01

It’s born in the imagination

The ease of picturing an ideal, set against the monstrous difficulty of building it. The sickness stagnates in that fertile gap.

02

The real fault: under-budgeting for difficulty

Our problems don’t arise from loving perfection — but from the reckless tendency to under-budget for what it costs. How well we budget depends on grasping the true, inherent difficulty of the task.

03

Premature perfectionism — the thing to criticise

High standards only turn toxic at one precise moment: when we expect something to be substantially easier than it is, and misread the struggle.

Same reality, two labels. The perfectionist picks the left — and pays for it.
04

Where it strikes — the under-budgeted timelines

Torture arrives when we imagine the summit is near, because we lack information on how hard others worked and how much they suffered to reach it.

Under-budgeted

A good novel in 6 months

The first draft is meant to be bad. Nobody told you.

Under-budgeted

A good career by 30

Measured against summaries, never against the years of grind behind them.

Under-budgeted

A good marriage, spontaneously

Worked out on the fly, with no model of how much it actually asks.

05

The cure: see the hidden labour

A better culture would endlessly show us the first drafts and hidden labours of others — and the true horrors exacted by anything worth doing.

The reframe worth keeping

The problem isn’t that we aim for perfection. It’s that we don’t have an accurate, redemptive idea of what perfection really demands.

Budget honestly for the difficulty → the weak beginning stops meaning anything about you. It’s difficult. You knew it would be.

06

The Perfectionist Trap

We aim at a career by admiring its masters — the airport architect, the star fund manager, the prize-winning novelist, the acclaimed chef. We form our plans on perfection. Then we take our first steps, and the trouble begins.

07

It isn’t your fault — the edited-out failure

The media quietly edits out billions of unremarkable lives, and the years of failure, rejection and frustration behind even the achievers — serving a curated reel of peak moments that then seems like the baseline, not the violent exception it really is.

We can’t forgive our early drafts — largely because we’ve never seen the early drafts of those we admire.

09

The deeper root: it isn’t about the work

There’s a person we admire — up at dawn, rarely on holiday, always another hour of work, constantly promoted, never a late or sub-stellar piece. “High standards,” we say. But the concern isn’t their work — its recipients are privileged. It’s the state of their soul.

▶ Watch · School of Life: perfectionism & self-worth ↗
Perfectionism doesn’t spring from loving perfection. It springs from a deficit of early love.
10

The Sisyphean engine — ill, not driven

To expunge the feeling, we’ll do almost anything: work all hours, curry favour, do twice the next person. One part of the mind keeps promising the other that the next achievement will finally bring peace.

We’re ill, not driven. We don’t want perfect work — we’re escaping a feeling. Work is only the medium in which we strive to grow tolerable to ourselves.
Why work can’t fix it

The problem didn’t begin with work

So work can never be its solution. The real goal isn’t to be the ideal employee — it’s to feel acceptable.

Why you can’t outsource it

Bosses · customers · capitalism will never let you rest

By their nature — no evil intent — they always demand more. Acceptance handed to them is never granted.

11

The shift: acceptance from the start

We need to move our sense of where the drive comes from. Not an unnatural love of working perfectly — an unusually intense impression that we are dreadful people. Working harder cannot be the answer.

A welcome sign

Being able to tolerate a little laziness

Not because you’re idle — but because you’ve learnt to speak more kindly to yourself.

A fair anger

Aim it where it belongs

At those who couldn’t, at the outset, accept you for who you were — without a surfeit of trophies and prizes.

The line to keep

Working well is an admirable goal — but it becomes a symptom the moment it turns into cover for a secret aspiration to correct a deficit of early love.

You deserved acceptance from the start. It was never your job to earn the right to exist.

In one line

Aim as high as you like — but budget for the true cost, and read your early struggle as the price of a long road, not a verdict on your worth.

Potential & Freedom

Potential isn’t a diamond buried under your fears — it’s the possible song of your particular instrument. You discover the instrument, remove the locks to open the window, and only the playing makes the song real. What clearing the locks gives you is not skill — it is freedom: presence, boldness, self-compassion, discipline, equanimity.

The Ladder of Stimulation

A spectrum from super-normal external hijackers (porn, reels, sugar) down to bare presence — ordered by how hard each spikes the reward system, how far it pulls you from the present, and how big a bill it leaves. Descend it one rung at a time, per context, as a marathon.

Time Approximation

A learning from my flat hunt. My default time estimates are inflated because I inherit them from unstructured experience — I assumed 7–10 days, but a structured sheet (sector → societies → brokers → flats) got me a flat on Day 1. The fix for a bad estimate isn't more effort; it's replacing the guess with structure.
