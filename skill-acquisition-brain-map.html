🧠 How one rep runs through the brain

  1. Hippocampus / MTL encodes the cue you were told → hands it up to working memory (dlPFC), which holds it "in mind" as the goal the movement is aiming at.
  2. dlPFC drives Primary Motor Cortex (M1) (effortfully, early) and the dorsomedial striatum picks the goal-directed action. M1 fires the movement — and sends a copy to the cerebellum.
  3. The cerebellum predicts the sensory result, compares it against your body's actual feedback and the intended goal, and sends the mismatch — the prediction error — back to M1 as a correction. That's the error-corrected rep.
  4. Dopamine (VTA/SNc) reports better- or worse-than-expected and trains the striatum to keep what worked.
  5. With reps + sleep, control migrates from the dorsomedial → dorsolateral striatum and the program consolidates in M1. The dlPFC steps back — the skill is now effortless (and re-engaging dlPFC now = choking).
