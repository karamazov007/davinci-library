Where my true passion lies

History — the Indian Subcontinent

I’ve always been passionate about history — and above all, the history of the Indian subcontinent: empires upon empires, wars upon wars, and the rulers who fought them. There are world-class channels for Roman, European and ancient history. For India’s, at that quality, there is almost nothing. That gap is the idea.

The opportunity
A gap the size of a subcontinent

Kings and Generals, Epic History TV, Historia Civilis, HistoryMarche and the rest have made world and European military history cinematic and beloved. No one has done the same for the subcontinent — for Panipat, Haldighati, Talikota, Plassey, Tarain, the Rajputs, the Marathas, the Cholas. The demand is proven; the supply is missing.

The scope
Empires, wars, and the timeline of it all

All the wars fought on Indian soil, the empires that rose and fell, the rulers and their campaigns — laid out as a proper timeline of the subcontinent. Not just what happened, but why it unfolded the way it did: the causes, the incentives, the turning points — first-principles history, not just narration.

The format
Borrow the best of every great channel

Animated campaign maps (Kings and Generals / HistoryMarche), cinematic storytelling (Epic History TV), clean causal analysis (Historia Civilis), immersion and reconstruction (Ancient Athens 3D / History in 3D), and OverSimplified’s accessible fun — fused, and pointed at India.

On the ground
Visit the ruins — then rebuild them

Go on-location to the forts, battlefields and ruins, then pair the footage with digital 3D recreations showing how each site stood in its prime — who built it, when, who broke it, and when. Revive the lost wonders from primary sources: show Nalanda and Vikramshila as living universities in their era, reconstructed from what the texts and archaeology tell us. Make the past you can walk through.

The edge
Use AI to make cinematic history affordable

The reason this hasn’t been done isn’t lack of stories — it’s the cost of maps, animation, 3D and research. AI changes the maths: AI-assisted map animation, 3D reconstruction, script research from sources, narration and visuals can produce a Kings-and-Generals-grade episode at a fraction of the time and money. That’s a modern advantage almost nobody is aiming at Indian history yet.
