# Staying Calm Under Surprise — Airway & Panic Control

*Blueprint for the L0 build (from discussion D1·1 + the heads-up case D1·2c). Full reasoning here;
the discussion map stays lightweight and just points to this file.*

---

## 1. The mechanism — why water in the nose hurts *and* scares

- The nasal passage is wired to the **trigeminal nerve**, which runs to the brain and **refers**
  its sensation up into the forehead and the top/back of the head. So an irritation *in the nose*
  is felt as a sharp sting "inside your head."
- That sensation is, in itself, **completely harmless**.
- But the brain *also* fires a hardwired alarm: **"water in the airway = I'm drowning."** This is
  what produces the panic — stiffening, clumsy/hardened movements, freeze — and the feeling of
  drowning **even in safe or shallow water**.
- The core conflict: a **harmless sensation** and a **drowning alarm** fire at the same time.
  Because your nervous system is currently *entrained* to treat water-in-nose as danger, the
  drowning story dominates over the (correct) knowledge that the sensation is harmless.

## 2. Contact vs. inhalation — two different events

- **Contact alone:** the moment water *touches* the nasal lining, the sting is felt. It happens
  even if you never inhale. The sting is largely because **pool/lake water is less salty than the
  nasal lining** (osmotic irritation), plus the trigeminal response.
- **Inhalation:** if you actually breathe the water *in*, you add **coughing, gagging and airway
  distress** on top of the sting. This is the genuinely unpleasant, escalating part.
- **Takeaway:** *not inhaling* prevents the dangerous part (water reaching the throat/lungs, the
  choke). It does **not** switch off the contact sting.

## 3. Prevention — defend the nose

- **Gentle outward nasal pressure:** a constant, subtle exhale out through the nose means water
  can't get in to touch the lining. Works whenever you have breath to spare (face-down, or during
  a stroke phase).
- **On your back you can't sustain that exhale** (you're mouth-breathing). So the back-float
  defence is: **nose to the sky** (a slight chin-tuck lifts the nostrils above the splash line) plus
  a **reactive puff** out the nose as a wave or a passing swimmer's wash arrives.

## 4. If prevention fails — the recovery (back float / treading)

Water is in, the lining is touched, the sting is felt. Then:

1. **Small puff out the nose** to clear the water — ideally *without* emptying your lungs (keep
   some air, so you don't lose buoyancy).
2. If you *do* exhale hard and empty out, your body dips (lungs are your main float), so **scull
   lightly with the hands** to hold yourself up through that moment.
3. **Quick inhale through the mouth** → **relax** → buoyancy is restored, hands can rest.
4. If it was a lot / you're destabilised: **convert to a treading position**, fully stabilise your
   nose, mouth and breathing there, then **return to the back float**.

**Sequence:** *puff → (light scull) → mouth-breathe → relax → (if needed) tread → stabilise → back to float.*

## 5. The same thing while heads-up breaststroke swimming

Very similar, and *easier*, because heads-up is already almost a tread (head up, hands sculling):

- **Minor** (a little water): ignore it / puff and **keep swimming**.
- **Moderate–hard** (a lot of water, a soft stinging pain): **convert heads-up → treading** (one
  small move), stabilise, then resume.
- **Anchor principle:** the tread is always one tiny move away, which is exactly why heads-up is a
  safe gear.

## 6. The cure — desensitization training (fear-extinction)

Prevention and workarounds manage the problem; **training dissolves it.**

- In **shallow water**, on purpose let a little water into the nose → **exhale it out through the
  nose** → **inhale through the mouth** → stabilise → let a little more in → repeat.
- **Small, calm, repeated** — stay **under the panic line** (overwhelming yourself can reinforce
  the fear instead of erasing it).
- In due course your mind learns the truth: **this is not an emergency — it's boring, a non-event.**
- Spread it over **sessions and days**, a few minutes each.

## 🎯 Cues

- **Back float / treading — water in the nose:** *"Exhale, scull, mouth-breathe, relax."*
- **If it's harder:** *"Exhale → scull → drop to a tread → mouth-breathe, relax → then switch/resume."*
- **Heads-up breaststroke:** minor → *puff & carry on*; moderate → *convert to a tread, clear, resume.*
- **Prevention:** *"Nose to the sky; subtle nasal exhale; puff as a wave comes."*
- **Training:** *"Small, calm, repeated — until it's boring, not until you're drained."*
