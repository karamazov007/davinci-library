## 1. Origin vs insertion — the terms, and how you decide which is which

Every muscle attaches to bone at (at least) two ends. The convention:

- **Origin** = the attachment on the **more fixed / stationary** bone (usually the more central,
  proximal end — the anchor). It doesn't move much when the muscle fires.
- **Insertion** = the attachment on the bone that **moves toward the origin** when the muscle
  shortens (usually the more distal / mobile end — the mover).

**How to decide:** ask "when this muscle contracts, which end stays put and which end travels?"
The end that travels = insertion; the anchor = origin. Rule of thumb: *the insertion moves toward
the origin.*

**Caveat (matters for lifting):** origin/insertion is a **convention**, not a law of physics.
Either end can be the mover depending on what's fixed. Example: a normal press moves the arm
(insertion) toward the fixed trunk; a push-up fixes the hands and moves the trunk toward the arm
("reverse action"). Same muscle, same shortening — just which end is anchored flips.

## 2. The muscles

### Pectoralis major — the visible chest (two heads)
One muscle, two heads, named by where they originate. **Both insert on the same spot on the
humerus** — the lateral lip of the intertubercular (bicipital) groove / crest of the greater
tubercle.

| Head | Origin (anchor, on the trunk) | Insertion (mover, on the arm) | Emphasis |
|------|-------------------------------|-------------------------------|----------|
| **Clavicular head** | medial half of the **clavicle** | humerus (bicipital groove) | "upper chest" — incline work |
| **Sternocostal head** | **sternum** + upper ~6 costal cartilages (+ external-oblique aponeurosis) | humerus (bicipital groove) | "mid/lower chest" — flat & decline work |

- So your read was right: clavicle→humerus and sternum→humerus. Both anchor on the **trunk**
  (fixed) and pull on the **humerus** (mover) → trunk = origin, humerus = insertion.
- Detail (not essential): the tendon **twists** at the humerus — the lower sternal fibers attach
  *higher* on the humerus and the upper clavicular fibers attach *lower*. Ignore for training.

### Pectoralis minor — the small one underneath
- **Origin:** anterior surface of **ribs 3–5**. **Insertion:** **coracoid process of the scapula**
  (not the humerus).
- **Function:** it acts on the **shoulder blade**, not the arm — it **protracts, depresses, and
  downwardly rotates the scapula** (pulls it forward and down against the ribcage); also assists
  forced inhalation.
- **Worth training? No — for size.** It's small, sits *under* the pec major (invisible), and can't
  be meaningfully isolated; it gets incidental work in any pressing/dip. The reason it matters is
  the opposite: a **tight/short pec minor** tilts the scapula forward → rounded shoulders and is a
  contributor to **shoulder impingement**. So the practical job for pec minor is **stretch /
  mobilize it**, not grow it. (Ties into the impingement topic.)

**Bottom line:** train the **pec major** (that's the chest you see and build). Leave the pec minor
to stretching/posture work.

## 3. Pec major's movements (functions) — the names

The pec major moves the **humerus at the shoulder (glenohumeral) joint**. Its actions:

1. **Horizontal adduction** (a.k.a. transverse adduction / horizontal flexion) — bringing the arm
   **across the front of the body**, from out-to-the-side to in-front. **This is the pec's
   signature action** and the basis of every chest exercise (flyes and presses). The whole pec does
   this.
2. **Shoulder flexion** — raising the arm **forward and up** from the side. Mostly the
   **clavicular head** (→ why incline hits the upper chest).
3. **Shoulder adduction / extension** — pulling the arm **down and in** toward the body, e.g. from
   overhead down to the side. Mostly the **sternocostal head** (→ why decline / dips hit the lower
   chest). The sternal head is a strong "pull the arm down" muscle.
4. **Internal (medial) rotation** of the humerus — turning the arm inward.

## 4. The master principle (fiber direction → movement → exercise)

You load a muscle by making it **do its job**. The pec's job is to bring the upper arm **across and
in toward the midline** (horizontal adduction) + rotate it inward. So **every** chest exercise is a
variation of moving the arm/elbow from *out-wide* to *in-and-together*.

Which head does most of the work is set by the **direction the arm travels**, because that lines up
with one head's fiber direction:

| Arm path | Fibers that align | Emphasis | Exercise angle |
|----------|-------------------|----------|----------------|
| up **and** in | clavicular (run up→down to the arm) | upper chest | **incline** |
| horizontally in | whole pec | mid chest | **flat** |
| down **and** in | sternocostal (run up→in from below) | lower chest | **decline / dips** |

This is the chest version of the pull-day "elbow path decides the target" rule — here it's
**"arm path decides which head."**

## 5. The 2-question decoder — reading any chest exercise

For **any** chest exercise, ask two things:

**Q1 — Press or fly?** (how pure is the horizontal adduction)
- **Press** = horizontal adduction **+ elbow extension** → triceps & front delt assist → can load heavy. Compound.
- **Fly** = elbow stays fixed, arms sweep in an arc → **pure** horizontal adduction, no triceps. Isolation.

**Q2 — Which way does the arm travel *relative to the torso*?** (which head)
The subtlety: arm relative to the **torso**, not the ground. Bench angle / cable anchor is just the
tool that sets this path.
- toward the **collarbone/head end** = up-and-in = **clavicular / upper**
- **straight across** = horizontal = **whole pec / mid**
- toward the **belly/feet end** = down-and-in = **sternal / lower**

### Mapping the common lifts

| Exercise | Press/Fly | Arm path vs torso | Head |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|------|
| **Incline DB/BB press** (~30°) | press | up-and-in | clavicular / **upper** |
| **Flat press** | press | straight across | whole pec / **mid** |
| **Decline press / dips** (lean fwd) | press | down-and-in | sternal / **lower** |
| **Cable fly — low anchor, sweep up** | fly | up-and-in | **upper** |
| **Cable fly — shoulder anchor** | fly | straight across | **mid** |
| **Cable fly — high anchor, sweep down** | fly | down-and-in | **lower** |
| **Pec deck** | fly | straight across (pads at shoulder ht) | **mid** |
| **Flat DB fly** | fly | straight across | mid (stretch-biased, see §6) |

### Push-ups — the naming inverts (and that proves the rule)
A push-up is a flat-press pattern with the **reverse action** (hands fixed, torso moves — the
origin/insertion flip from §1). Because it's **arm-relative-to-torso** that matters:
- **Feet elevated ("decline" push-up)** → head low → arms travel toward the head end → **UPPER** chest (like an incline press).
- **Hands elevated ("incline" push-up)** → head high → arms travel toward the feet end → **LOWER** chest (like a decline press).

So the labels are the **opposite** of the presses with the same name. Trust the arm-path rule, not
the label. (Wider hands = more pec ROM; narrower = more triceps.)

## 6. Squeeze vs stretch — where the tension actually is (resistance profile)

**Two different ideas people jam together:**
1. **Stretch vs squeeze = muscle LENGTH** at the two ends of the range. Stretch = pec longest
   (arms wide/open). Squeeze = pec shortest (arms crossed in front). This is just *position*.
2. **Where the exercise is HARD = the resistance profile.** This is *not* the same as length — it's
   set by the **direction of the resistance vs the position of the arm**.

**The one principle:** a muscle is only loaded where the resistance **points against the muscle's
job**. The pec's job is to pull the arm **across and in** — so the pec only gets tension where
something is trying to pull the arm **back apart**.
- **Gravity (DB/BB/bodyweight)** always pulls **straight down** — you can't aim it.
- **Cables** pull **along the cable line** — you aim it by choosing the anchor.

### Why the dumbbell fly loses tension at the squeeze (top)
- **Bottom (stretch):** arms out wide, roughly horizontal. Gravity pulls the dumbbells down and the
  pec has to haul the arms up-and-across against it, at a long lever → **high tension, right at the
  stretch.** (This is the DB fly's strength.)
- **Top (squeeze):** arms vertical, dumbbells stacked over the shoulders. Gravity now pulls
  **straight down through the arm into the joint/bones** — it is no longer trying to pull the arms
  *apart*. Nothing resists the horizontal-adduction "squeeze," so the pec can switch off. → **~zero
  tension at the squeeze.** The "squeeze" you feel is you consciously flexing, not the load.

### Why the cable fly keeps tension the whole way
The cables pull the arms **outward toward the pulleys** — that opposes horizontal adduction across
the *entire* range.
- **Stretch (arms wide):** cables pull the arms further apart → pec resists. ✓
- **Squeeze (arms crossed):** cables are *still* pulling the arms back apart → the pec must keep
  working hard to hold them together → **tension stays high at the squeeze.** ✓

### The self-check cue (works on any exercise)
> "If I relaxed the muscle **right now**, would the weight move in the direction my muscle resists?"

- Top of a **DB fly**: relax → the arms wouldn't fall apart (gravity holds them up over the
  shoulders) → **no tension**.
- Top of a **cable fly**: relax → the arms would fly back apart → **tension**.

### The "why" underneath: moment arm (leverage)
Tension on the muscle = weight × **moment arm** (how far the line of resistance passes from the
joint — its leverage over you).
- DB fly top: gravity's line passes ~through the shoulder → moment arm ≈ 0 → no torque → no tension.
- DB fly bottom: gravity's line is way out to the side → big moment arm → big torque → high tension.
- Cable: the line stays ~perpendicular to the arm the whole way (you set the anchor) → moment arm
  stays large → tension stays high throughout.

### Practical payoff
- The DB fly isn't "bad" — it's **stretch-biased** (its hard point is the stretch), and loading the
  stretch is especially good for growth. Its blind spot is only the squeeze.
- The cable fly / pec deck cover the **squeeze**.
- So pair them: **stretch-biased move (DB fly, deep press) + squeeze-biased move (cable fly, pec
  deck)** = full tension across the whole range. That's *why both exist.*
- Same idea explains presses: free-weight presses are hard at the bottom (stretch) and easy at
  lockout (bones stack, pec rests — the joint-stacking idea from pull day); cables/machines can
  keep tension nearer the top.

### Resistance-profile curve — flat DB press vs horizontal cable fly (over 3 reps)
Idealized teaching curves (shape matters, not exact %). Y = % of that exercise's own peak pec
tension; X = position walking stretch → squeeze → stretch, three reps.

- **Flat dumbbell press (gravity):** spikes to ~100% at every **stretch** (bottom), crashes to
  ~10–15% at every **squeeze / lockout** (top). Three dead zones per set where the bones stack and
  the pec rests. Tension lives at the **bottom / stretch**.
- **Horizontal cable fly (cable pulls arms apart the whole way):** never drops below ~60%, and
  **peaks at the squeeze** (arms crossed) because the cable still fights the adduction. Tension held
  across the whole rep, biased to the **squeeze**.
- **Verdict:** DB press = stretch-loaded with lockout gaps; cable fly = squeeze-loaded, no gaps.
  Neither covers the full range alone → pair a stretch-biased move with a squeeze-biased one.

## 7. Exercises in detail

### Incline dumbbell press (upper pec, press = stretch-loaded)
**Compressed cue:** *"Pinned blades, stretch low, press up-and-in, don't reach at the top."*

- **Bench angle:** ~30° (2nd notch). Steeper (45°+) → front delts steal it.
- **Get into position:** dumbbells on thighs → kick each up with the thigh as you lie back. Finish
  by pulling them in, chin tucked, sit up with momentum. Never lower heavy DBs straight to the floor.
- **Shoulder blades (the anchor):** pull **down & back, pin to the pad, keep pinned all set** —
  including the top. #1 thing: stable base, safe shoulder, chest does the work.
  - *Set = depression (down, away from ears) + retraction (blades gently squeezed together), done
    as ONE motion, then held.* It's a **moderate** set, not a max row-squeeze — set it once and just
    don't let it slip (like planting your feet). Of the two, **depression ("down") matters most for
    shoulder safety**; retraction gives the stable shelf + lifts the chest.
- **Shoulders:** back & down, never shrug to the ears. Chest proud.
- **Head/neck:** rest on the pad, neutral, don't crane to watch the DBs.
- **Back:** upper back flat & pinned; only a **small** natural lumbar curve (a big arch flattens the
  torso → turns it into a flat press).
- **Hips:** glutes stay on the **flat seat**, back on the incline pad — don't climb hips onto the
  incline, don't lift the hips to heave.
- **Legs/feet:** flat on floor, ~shoulder-width, **drive feet into the ground** (leg drive) to stay
  tight. Plate under feet if on tiptoes.
- **The rep:** start DBs at upper chest, forearms vertical, wrists over elbows, **elbows ~45–60°
  from torso** (arrow, not a flared T — flare = shoulder stress/impingement). Lower to a deep
  **stretch** (DBs go deeper than a bar; don't bang shoulders). Press **up-and-in** toward each
  other over the upper chest; don't clang them. Think "push chest into the DBs."
- **Lockout? NO.** Keep a slight bend at top (bones stack at full lockout → chest tension drops) and
  **never let the blades roll forward / shoulders reach** to lock out (dumps load on the joint).
- **Breathing:** inhale down (fill the chest, brace), exhale up through the sticking point.
- **Weight:** start light (DBs wobble). First working set ~10–12 clean reps, ~2 RIR, controlled
  eccentric. Wobble/drift = too heavy. Double progression: 8–12 range, add reps → then add load.
- **Warm-up:** general 5 min (arm circles, band pull-aparts, shoulder prep) + ramp sets
  **"half → three-quarters → go"** (½ wt ×8, ¾ wt ×5, then working set), not to failure.
- **Gym cue card:** (1) blades down/back/pinned (2) butt on seat, feet driving (3) elbows 45°,
  forearms vertical (4) stretch low, press up-and-in (5) stop short of lockout, don't let shoulders
  reach.

### Machine chest press (mid pec, press — stable, push it hard)
**Compressed cue:** *"Handles at mid-chest, blades pinned, press forward-and-together, don't reach."*
**Value:** fixed-path + balanced → safe to take close to / to failure without a spotter. This is the
"grind hard" exercise.

- **Seat height = make-or-break:** set so **handles line up with mid-chest** (sternum/lower-pec).
  Too low → presses up into shoulders; too high → presses down. If start depth is adjustable, set a
  comfortable chest stretch with elbows ~torso level, not jammed behind.
- **Back:** flat on the pad, chest up, blades in contact.
- **Shoulder blades:** **down & back, moderate set, pinned & held** (same as incline DB press).
- **Shoulders:** back & down, never roll forward to reach — especially at lockout.
- **Head/neck:** on the headrest / neutral, eyes forward.
- **Hips:** sit fully back, glutes in seat.
- **Feet:** flat, planted, **driving the floor**. Use the foot lever (if any) to get set, then plant.
- **Grip:** wrists neutral & stacked (don't bend back); **neutral grip usually easier on shoulder**.
- **The rep:** elbows ~45° (not a flared T). Start = chest stretch (felt in chest, not front of
  shoulder). Press **forward & slightly together**, "push through the chest." Control the return —
  resist the stack, don't let it yank you back.
- **Lockout? Near-full, slight bend, keep blades set — don't round shoulders to reach.** (Advanced:
  a tiny controlled protraction maxes the squeeze — skip for now given shoulder focus.)
- **Breathing:** inhale back (stretch), exhale press forward.
- **Weight:** it's safe → push harder. ~8–12 reps, working sets ~1–2 RIR, **last set can be true
  failure**. Yanks you forward / can't control the return = too heavy. Double progression.
- **Warm-up:** shoulder prep + ramp "half → three-quarters → go".
- **Key difference vs DB press:** set the seat right, then *use the machine's safety to actually
  train to failure* — this is where you cash in the hard sets.

#### Refinements (from Q&A)
- **"Back flat" = blades/upper back on the pad, NOT spine pancaked.** Pinning blades + proud chest
  *does* create a slight arch — that's correct and wanted. Contact points: blades + glutes.
- **Don't scoot forward off the pad** — upper back/blades must stay in contact (that's the anchor);
  lower back arching slightly off is fine.
- **Feet:** plates/platform under them if they don't reach the floor — need a driving base.
- **Wrist straight:** knuckles → wrist → forearm in one line; weight over the heel of the palm.
  Never let the wrist cock back (extend) under load.
- **Stretch depth:** back until hands ~chest level / elbows ~torso plane = a good pec stretch.
  **Self-limit: stop the instant the stretch starts to break your blade set / pull the shoulders
  forward.** Depth = "how far while blades stay pinned," not the machine's full range.
- **Press is stretch-loaded** (hardest near the bottom) — a cam can even it out somewhat, but still
  stretch-biased vs a fly.
- **Bracing vs breath-hold (two things):** *brace* = belly breath + push the abdominal wall OUT
  (whole corset: TVA + obliques + front + diaphragm/pelvic floor), "about to take a punch" — NOT
  sucking in. Keep the brace across all reps. *Breath-hold (Valsalva)* = also sealing the air in →
  spikes BP → keep short, heavy sets only, stop if dizzy. Moderate reps: stay braced but exhale
  (controlled hiss) through the sticking point rather than holding one big breath for 2–3 reps.

#### Gym cue list (final)
**Set-up:** (1) seat → handles at mid-chest · (2) sit back, blades/upper back on pad, chest up
(slight arch ok) · (3) blades down & back, set & pinned (down matters most) · (4) feet flat &
driving · (5) wrists straight.
**Each rep:** (6) belly breath, brace (push out, don't suck in) · (7) control back to a stretch —
stop just before the blades un-set · (8) press forward-and-together, push through the chest ·
(9) near-full, slight bend, don't round the shoulders to reach · (10) exhale/hiss through the
sticking point, stay braced.
**One-liner:** *"Handles at mid-chest, blades pinned, brace, stretch to just before they slip,
press forward-and-together, don't reach."*

### Horizontal seated cable fly (mid pec, fly = squeeze-loaded)
**Compressed cue:** *"Fixed elbows, hug it in, squeeze — open only to where the blades stay pinned."*
**Purpose:** the **squeeze-loaded** partner to the presses (presses = stretch, this = contraction).
Usually done LAST in the chest day. Chase the squeeze here.

- **Set-up:** both pulleys at **shoulder height** (= horizontal = mid pec; lower→upper, higher→
  lower). Seat between the towers, cables coming from slightly behind/beside at shoulder level.
  Single-D handle each hand. **Start light** — isolation + vulnerable stretch; no ego-loading.
- **Torso:** upright or very slight hip lean, chest up & proud, light brace, don't round.
- **Shoulder blades:** down & back, set, pinned. Resist the roll-forward as hands meet (tiny
  controlled protraction at the very end ok; default pinned).
- **Shoulders:** down, no shrug. **Head/neck:** neutral. **Feet:** flat, driving.
- **Elbows: slight FIXED bend (~10–20°), NEVER changes** — #1 rule. Bend/straighten = it's a press,
  triceps take over. All motion at the shoulder.
- **Start (stretch):** arms out at shoulder height, hands ~in line with torso, blades pinned. Open
  only **until just before the stretch breaks the blade set** = your depth.
- **Squeeze:** sweep forward & together in a wide arc ("hug a tree"), lead with the upper
  arms/elbows (not the hands), hands meet/lightly cross in front of mid-chest, **squeeze hard** —
  this is where the cable keeps tension. (Optional: "pinkies toward each other" at the top.)
- **Return:** control back out, resist the cable into the stretch.
- **No lockout** (it's a fly) — end range = full squeeze; don't shrug/round to get closer.
- **"Fixed elbow" — what really must not change:** the invariant is that the **shoulder drives the
  motion and the pec does the work**, not the literal elbow angle. Fixed elbow is just the foolproof
  way to guarantee that. A **small, controlled elbow extension at the very peak** (after the shoulder
  has finished adducting) to reach a fuller squeeze is a legit hybrid — trades a little isolation for
  triceps/front-delt help. Failure mode: extending early / through the range / as the main mover →
  it's now a press (lost pec isolation + stretch). Same principle as the pull-day cable pullover.
- **Breathing:** light brace, exhale squeezing in, inhale opening out.
- **Weight:** ~12–15 reps, 1–2 RIR, feel the pec, control both ends. Lean/heave/elbow-bend = too
  heavy. Double progression.
- **Warm-up:** usually last (chest already warm) → one light feel-out set. If done first, add
  shoulder prep + a light ramp set.
- **Gym cue card:** (1) pulleys at shoulder height, sit tall, chest up (2) blades down/back, pinned
  (3) soft FIXED elbow bend (4) open to a stretch, stop before blades un-set (5) hug forward &
  together, squeeze hard.
