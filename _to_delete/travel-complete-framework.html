The Complete Trip Framework

Once the destination is chosen, every trip reduces to four systems. Here is the whole thing — mapped, not written.

✦At a glance

A destination tells you where. These four systems tell you how the trip actually happens.

MOVE+STAY+EXPERIENCE+EAT

Buy a package and these are bundled for you. Go DIY and you solve each one yourself. Either way, every trip is made of exactly these four.

🚗1 · Move

Two completely different scales: getting in & out of the destination, and moving around once there.

🏨2 · Stay

Not "which hotel?" first, but "how many operational bases, and where?"

🎯3 · Experience

Don't start from "what attractions exist?" Start from "what experiences do we want?"

🍽4 · Eat

Food is a logistics problem with two purposes: fuel vs experience.

🔗How the four interact

The systems are not independent — each one constrains the next.

Experience → Move. "Trek at 7 AM" forces a base near the trailhead.
Stay → Experience. A hotel 30 km out quietly kills operationally-painful experiences.
Experience → Eat. A 6-hour trek means breakfast → packed lunch → snack → dinner.
Move → Eat. A 4-hour drive turns lunch into a transit meal, not a destination.

⚙The planning engine

Run the four in this order — then iterate.

📦Package vs DIY

The key insight: a package doesn't create new categories — it just bundles the execution.

Either way, the four systems are the same. A package outsources the operational layer; DIY keeps it. The destination told you where — these four are always how.
