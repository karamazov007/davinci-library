## 1. The muscles this day covers

**Back = four jobs, not one muscle:**
- **Lats** (latissimus dorsi) — width / the V-taper. Job: bring the arm from overhead down to the side (adduction) + pull the elbow down/back (extension).
- **Mid-back** (rhomboids + mid/lower traps) — thickness. Job: retract the shoulder blades (pull them together).
- **Rear delts** — back of the shoulder. Missed by pressing, recover fast → need direct work.
- **Lower back (erectors) + upper traps** — covered by the big compound pulls / deadlifts (little direct work needed).

**Biceps = long head + short head + brachialis:**
- **Long head** — outer head, gives the "peak." Emphasised with the arm *behind* the body (stretched).
- **Short head** — inner head. Emphasised with the arm *forward*/supported.
- **Brachialis** — sits *under* the biceps, pushes it up → adds arm thickness. Plus **brachioradialis** (forearm). Trained with neutral/reverse grip.

**Framing note:** you can *bias* a region (via line of pull + where the muscle is stretched), not perfectly isolate it. Evidence base = full ROM + load the stretched position (Schoenfeld et al.) + biomechanics; synthesised by evidence-based coaches (Nippard, Israetel, Henselmans); matches the hub's Muscle Atlas rankings.

## 2. The 7-exercise day (in order)

| # | Exercise | Targets | Notes |
|---|----------|---------|-------|
| 1 | **Lat pulldown** (or pull-up) | **Lats — width** | Vertical pull, done fresh. Full stretch at top, drive elbow toward the hip. |
| 2 | **Chest-supported row** | **Mid-back thickness** (rhomboids, mid traps) + lats | Chest support removes lower-back fatigue & cheating. Retract the shoulder blades. |
| 3 | **Cable lat pullover** | **Lats — isolated, deep stretch** | Arms overhead, fixed elbows, sweep down. Loaded stretch + constant cable tension; no biceps. (Chosen over straight-arm pulldown for the deeper stretch — see §5.) |
| 4 | **Face pull / reverse pec-deck** | **Rear delts** | Elbows high; feel the back of the shoulder. |
| 5 | **Incline dumbbell curl** | **Biceps long head** (peak), stretched | Lying back puts the arm behind you → stretches the long head. |
| 6 | **Cable or preacher curl** | **Biceps short head** (inner) | Arm forward/supported; constant tension (cable) or hard bottom (preacher). |
| 7 | **Hammer curl** | **Brachialis** (arm thickness) + **brachioradialis** (forearm) | Neutral grip. |

---

## 3. Ordering principle

Heavy compounds while fresh → isolation → rear delts → **biceps LAST**.
Biceps are the assisting / weak-link muscle in every row and pulldown, so you don't
pre-fatigue them before the big back work (same logic as triceps-last on push day).

---

## 4. Supersets

Rule: **only superset two moves that don't share the working muscles or grip** — else both suffer.
- ✅ **#3 cable pullover + #5 incline curl** — pullover uses no biceps, curl uses no lats → don't compete.
- ✅ **#4 rear delts + #7 hammer curl** — rear delts & biceps barely overlap.
- ❌ **Never superset #1 lat pulldown + #2 chest-supported row** — both hammer back, biceps & grip; do them as **straight sets**, full rest, full effort.

---

## 5. Exercise-choice calls we made

- **Cable lat pullover vs straight-arm pulldown** → **cable lat pullover** (both cable/constant tension, but the pullover loads a deeper overhead stretch = better growth stimulus). Tiebreaker: do whichever you actually *feel* in your lats — a movement you feel beats a "better" one you don't. Fall back to straight-arm pulldown if you can't feel the pullover.
- **Cable lat pullover vs dumbbell pullover** → cable (dumbbell version loses tension at the top and leaks into the chest).
- **Keep the hammer curl** — not redundant with the incline curl; it hits the brachialis + brachioradialis, which the supinated curls miss.

---

## 6. Volume flag

Three direct curls (5, 6, 7) **plus** the big indirect biceps hit from moves 1–3 = a lot of biceps.
Great for an arm-emphasis day, but if the elbows complain, **drop #6 first**.
General principle: back is the prime mover here (most volume); biceps get topped-up direct work
because the pulls already fed them (like triceps on push day).

## 7. Width vs thickness (arm + back) — what builds each & why both matter

**The general rule — width vs thickness = your viewing angle:**
- **Width** = side-to-side spread seen **from the front** (the outline/silhouette).
- **Thickness** = front-to-back depth seen **from the side** (how much it sticks *out*).

### The arm — it's NOT mainly the biceps
- **Triceps ≈ 2/3 of upper-arm size** — the biggest lever for a *thick* arm. Biceps alone never builds a big arm.
- **Biceps** (front) — the front bulge + the **peak** (long head).
- **Brachialis** (under + outer side of the biceps) — pushes the biceps up AND adds **front-on width** / fullness (hammer curls / neutral grip).
- **Viewing:** front-on = the arm's *width* (biceps + brachialis bulge out to the sides); side-on = the arm's *depth* (biceps front + triceps back).
- **Complete arm = triceps (size) + biceps (front peak) + brachialis (outer/width).**

### The back — the classic width-vs-thickness split
| Dimension | Muscle | Built by | Seen from | Aesthetic role |
|-----------|--------|----------|-----------|----------------|
| **Width** (V-taper) | **Lats** | **Vertical pulls** (pulldown, pull-up, cable pullover) | Front (the flare) | Broad-shouldered, small-waisted *frame / outline* |
| **Thickness** (density) | **Mid-back** (traps, rhomboids) | **Horizontal rows** (chest-supported row, cable row) | Side / back pose | Dense, 3-D *fill* |

**Why both:** width = the impressive *outline* (V-taper is the #1 aesthetic cue); thickness = the *fill/density*. Wide-but-flat looks like a cutout; thick-but-narrow looks stubby. Both → complete from every angle.

### Why the 7-exercise day already covers both
- **Width work** = the vertical pulls (#1 pulldown, #3 cable pullover) → lats.
- **Thickness work** = the horizontal row (#2 chest-supported row) → mid-back.
- Rule of thumb: a good back day always pairs a **vertical pull (width)** with a **horizontal row (thickness)**. Same logic for arms: train **triceps + biceps + brachialis**, not just curls.

## 8. Important: the width/thickness framework fits the BACK, not the arm

Width vs thickness is clean for the **back** because two *different muscle groups*, seen from two
*different angles*, create the two dimensions. The **biceps is basically one muscle**, so its heads
don't make "width" vs "thickness" — they change its **shape**. Don't force the framework onto the arm.

**What the arm exercises actually build (shape + size, not width/thickness):**
- **Incline dumbbell curl (long head)** → the **peak** (height of the mound when flexed).
- **Cable / preacher curl (short head)** → **inner fullness** (rounder/fuller biceps from the front — closest thing to front-on "width").
- **Hammer curl (brachialis)** → pushes the biceps **up** (more apparent peak) + fills the **outer arm** (real arm width/size).
- **The arm's true thickness/size ≈ the TRICEPS (~2/3 of the arm) — and those live on PUSH day**, not pull day. On pull day you build biceps *shape* + a little arm size (brachialis); the arm's bulk comes from triceps.

**The two frameworks side by side:**

| | Back | Arm |
|---|------|-----|
| Framework | **Width vs Thickness** | **Overall size + biceps shape** |
| "Width"-ish | Lats (vertical pulls) | short head + brachialis (front-on fullness) |
| "Thickness"/size | Mid-back (rows) | **Triceps (push day!)** + brachialis |
| Extra | — | Long head = the *peak* (height) |

**Bottom line:** Back → width (lats) vs thickness (mid-back), two clean dimensions.
Arm → overall size (triceps-led, on push day) + biceps shape (long head = peak, short head = fullness,
brachialis = height + width). The two don't parallel — the arm has no distinct "width vs thickness" split.

## 9. Arm muscle heads & attachments (origins → insertions)

**Naming mnemonic:** the prefix = the number of heads. **bi**ceps = 2, **tri**ceps = 3, **quad**riceps = 4.

### Biceps brachii — 2 heads (long, short)
Both start on the **SCAPULA**, both insert on the **RADIUS** — the biceps *skips the humerus entirely*.

| Head | Origin | Insertion |
|------|--------|-----------|
| **Long head** | supraglenoid tubercle (scapula) | radial tuberosity (radius) |
| **Short head** | coracoid process (scapula) | radial tuberosity (radius) |

- Crosses **shoulder + elbow** (two-joint) → arm-behind stretches the long head (**incline curl**).
- Inserts on the radius (a rotating bone) → strong **supinator** (turns palm up).
- The long-head tendon *passes through* a groove in the humerus but does **not** attach there.

### Brachialis — a SEPARATE muscle (NOT a biceps head)
- **Origin:** front of the lower **humerus** → **Insertion:** **ulna** (coronoid process).
- Crosses **only the elbow**. Pure elbow flexor; adds arm width/thickness (hammer / neutral-grip curls).

### Triceps brachii — 3 heads (long, lateral, medial)
Three different origins, **one shared insertion** at the **olecranon of the ulna** (the point of the elbow).

| Head | Origin | Insertion |
|------|--------|-----------|
| **Long head** | infraglenoid tubercle (**scapula**) | olecranon (ulna) |
| **Lateral head** | posterior **humerus**, above the spiral groove | olecranon (ulna) |
| **Medial head** | posterior **humerus**, below the spiral groove (deep) | olecranon (ulna) |

- **Long head** crosses **shoulder + elbow** → stretched with the arm **overhead** → **overhead extensions** target it.
- **Lateral & medial** cross **only the elbow** → pure elbow extensors (pushdowns).
- (The *medial head* is just a head of the triceps — not to be confused with the **coracobrachialis**, a separate front-of-arm shoulder flexor.)

**Compartment cheat-sheet:** front of upper arm (flexors) = biceps + brachialis + coracobrachialis · back (extensor) = triceps.

## 10. Bones that matter for lifting (reference)

**Mindset:** you don't memorise all ~206 bones. You care about bones as **anchor points**
(where muscles attach — origins & insertions) and the **landmarks form cues use** ("bar over
midfoot," "sit back into the hips," "chest up"). That's ~15 major bones + a few landmarks.

**Upper body**
- **Sternum + ribs (ribcage)** — chest attaches here; also the platform the scapula glides on.
- **Clavicle + scapula** — the **shoulder girdle**; connects the arm to the rest of the skeleton.
- **Humerus** (upper arm) → **radius + ulna** (forearm).

**Spine** — vertebral column (cervical / thoracic / lumbar). Neutral spine, bracing.

**Lower body** (the big muscles live here — don't skip these)
- **Pelvis / hip bone** — glutes, hamstrings, hip flexors, abs all anchor here. Home of the hip hinge, anterior/posterior pelvic tilt, and the **sit bones** (ischial tuberosities = hamstring origin). It's the **pelvic girdle** connecting the leg to the spine.
- **Femur** (thigh) — largest bone; quads, hamstrings, glutes, adductors attach.
- **Patella** (kneecap) — the quad tendon wraps over it.
- **Tibia + fibula** (shin + outer lower leg) — quads (via patellar tendon), hamstrings, calves.

**The mirror that makes it easy to remember — arm ↔ leg use the same blueprint:**

| | Girdle (connects to body) | Upper segment (1 bone) | Lower segment (2 bones) | End |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Upper limb** | shoulder girdle (clavicle + scapula) | humerus | radius + ulna | hand |
| **Lower limb** | pelvic girdle (pelvis) | femur | tibia + fibula | foot |

One bone up top, two below, held to the body by a girdle — same top and bottom.

## 11. Execution & cues (brief)

### The master principle
**Elbow path decides the target** (grip mostly just follows it):
- **Elbows flared out + squeeze the shoulder blades together** → mid-back (rhomboids, mid-traps) = **thickness**.
- **Elbows driven down & in toward the hips (not flared)** → lats = **width**.
- Why: you load a muscle by doing *its job* — squeezing the blades together IS the rhomboid/trap action; pulling the arm down to the side IS the lat action.

| Move | Job | Elbow path |
|------|-----|-----------|
| Chest-supported row | mid-back thickness | flared out, pull high to sternum, **squeeze blades** |
| Lat pulldown | lat width | driven **down & in** to the hips |

### Chest-supported dumbbell row
- Bench **~30°** (flatter = more mid/upper back; steeper = more lats). Dumbbells must clear the floor for full ROM.
- **Neutral grip** (natural for dumbbells — don't force pronation; barbell/machine versions use overhand).
- Chest **glued to the pad the whole set**, flare elbows ~45°, pull to sternum, **squeeze blades at top**, let blades spread for a stretch at the bottom, control the negative.
- **Fault fixes:** chest lifting off the pad = momentum/heaving = **too heavy** → lighten it. Can't fully squeeze the blades = too heavy (can't finish the retraction) + still-developing skill → lighten until chest stays pinned AND you can pinch the blades hard.

### Lat pulldown — the 5 cues (in order)
1. **Lean & lock** — slight back lean (~20–30°), chest up, hold it fixed (don't swing/heave).
2. **Stretch at the top** — full overhead reach, let the shoulder blades rise.
3. **Elbows down & in** to your hips / "back pockets," *not flared* ← the money cue for lats.
4. **Bar to upper chest** (not behind the neck).
5. **Own both directions** — control the pull AND resist the negative.
- Compressed cue: *"Lean, stretch, elbows to back pockets, control it down."*
- Grip: medium-wide overhand or neutral, **relaxed** hook grip (don't death-grip). A *small* controlled torso lean is OK for range; a big rock = cheat.

### Grip — three separate things
- **Wrap:** full grip (thumb around, opposite fingers) vs **thumbless / false / "suicide" grip** (thumb alongside the fingers) → choose for feel/mind-muscle. **Hard rule: always full grip when the weight is over your body/head** (barbell bench, overhead press) + on heavy loads (or use straps).
- **Orientation (matters for shoulders):** thumb-up = **"full can"** (safe, clears impingement) · thumb-down/pinky-up = **"empty can"** (pinches the rotator-cuff tendon).
- **Palm direction:** pronated (overhand) · supinated (underhand) · neutral (palms facing).

### Cable lat pullover — how to do it
- **High pulley**, straight bar or rope, **overhand grip** ~shoulder-width, **relaxed** grip.
- **Hinge forward at the hips ~45°**, flat back, chest up, core braced. Start with arms extended up toward the pulley (feel the lat stretch).
- **Slight, FIXED elbow bend** — sweep the straight arms down in an **arc to your thighs** (motion is pure **shoulder extension**), feel the lats squeeze; control back up to the stretch.
- **#1 rule: elbows never bend/straighten** — the instant they do it becomes a triceps pushdown and the lats lose tension.
- Cues: lead with the lats/armpits · hinge & brace · full ROM (stretch up, squeeze down) · no momentum.
- Compressed cue: *"Hinge, straight arms, sweep down with the shoulder — elbows never move."*

### Lat anatomy (why the pullover works)
- Lat spans **trunk/pelvis/spine → humerus** (insertion on the humerus), crossing the **shoulder** but **not the elbow**.
- Arms overhead = lat **stretched** (long); pull down = lat **contracts** → shoulder extension/adduction (arm comes down to the side).
- Any move that brings the arm from overhead down to the body trains the lats (pulldown, pull-up, pullover). Straight-arm moves (**cable pullover / straight-arm pulldown**) *isolate* the lat because there's no elbow bend — pure shoulder motion.
